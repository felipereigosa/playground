## Claude

**Bow and arrow.** It is on your list, and it fits what is already there better than anything else:

- **The targets get a second use.** `hits.js` already handles a strike, so an arrow is a new projectile, not a new system. Arrows that stay stuck in the rings would be the new part.
- **It is a different two-handed feel from the blaster.** One hand holds the bow, the other takes the string, pulls and lets go; the draw length sets the speed. The blaster is hold and spray, this is one careful shot at a time.
- **Most of the rest exists.** Throwing and gravity come from the bowling ball, and the string's creak, twang and the thud can be made in `sound.js` like the others.
- **The risk is the draw**, since there is no resistance in the hand. Controller vibration that rises with the pull usually covers that, and it only shows on the Quest, not in the emulator.

Two others I would consider:

- **Gumball machine**: giant coin in the slot, turn the key, balls that bounce out. It is a toy mechanism in the spirit of the radio, and reuses its dial turn and the bowling physics. More modelling, less new code.
- **Clock with hands and alarm**: you just moved it back out of "done" in `ideas.txt`. It is the smallest of the three, and the alarm could use the radio's button rule and the sound system.

## Claude

Fixed in the export, and re-exported: reload on the Quest and look at the barrels. I have only checked it in a Blender render, not in the game.

**The cause** is a few very large faces on the barrels, of 17 to 34 sides, left where the strip, the button and the other insets were cut in. Such a face bends round the barrel but has a single normal, so the smooth shading creases around it.

**Weighted normals did not help.** I tried it on every part and rendered it next to the original with a glossy matcap: the creases stayed and the barrels gained visible lengthwise bands.

**What worked** was cutting every face of more than four sides into triangles. In the same comparison the diagonal crease on the rear barrel and the one along the front barrel disappear and the highlights run clean. The export script now does this to its own copies of the meshes. It costs nothing in the game, since the exporter turns everything into triangles anyway.

Your file is unchanged, so Blender's viewport still shows the creases. If you want them gone there too, select the big faces on the barrels and triangulate them (Ctrl+T), or join their corners to the neighbouring edge loops.
